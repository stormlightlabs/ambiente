# Three Studies

These pieces test whether a small amount of authored material can sustain an
identity while a process develops it. Each player below builds the canonical
piece in Rust, queries its events through WebAssembly, and uses the same browser
audio runtime as Studio.

Listen past the first cycle. Select **Next variation** while the piece is playing
to change its seed without changing its material or process. **Download for
Studio** saves the current variation as an `.ambiente.json` document.

## Phase

Phase copies one four-note phrase into exact 17.2, 23.8, and 31.1 second cycles.
The voices begin together, separate, and meet in new combinations without a
shared metric grid. Tempo changes do not alter these absolute-time clocks.

Listen for the phrase rather than an individual voice. Its contour remains
recognizable as the three registers exchange foreground and background.

## Drone

Drone repeats four sparse, absolute-time phrases over a two-minute span. Long
notes leave gaps between low tones, fifths, a glass halo, and filtered noise.
Low-probability omissions and octave changes alter each pass, while every event
comes from the same restrained pitch field.

Listen for silence as part of the form. A new seed should change entrances and
register without turning the piece into a busier texture.

## Pattern

Pattern starts with a syncopated sixteen-step cell and a separate broken pulse.
One voice states the cell without stochastic changes. Three copies reverse,
rotate, stretch, transpose, and omit events. Because each resulting event still
points to an authored matrix cell, editing the source matrix in Studio changes
the whole system.

Listen for the six-onset contour in the felt piano. The glass, pluck, and
percussion voices can vary by seed, but that cell should remain the rhythmic and
melodic reference.

## Compare the seeds

For each study, try at least three adjacent seeds and listen for several minutes.
A useful variation changes density, register, or alignment while preserving the
piece you chose. If a seed replaces identity with surprise, reduce the process
probability or simplify the authored material instead of adding another control.
